02 How I work

I take on a few projects at a time. Most start the same way: something runs on paper, a group chat or one person's memory, and it should run on software instead.

  • Tools for a business

    Booking, pricing, scheduling and the job board: the software your staff use at the counter and on their phones.

    Like Ludi and Wiseprint

  • Apps people carry

    iOS and Android apps over a backend you own, with maps, payments and roles when the job needs them.

    Like Cinna and Aristophanes

  • Hardware that talks to software

    Firmware, board bring-up and test tooling, and the app or dashboard on top of the device.

    Like Augur

  1. I

    A first call

    What it's for, who will use it, and what has to work on day one.

  2. II

    A written plan

    Requirements and the data model on paper, and the riskiest part tried first, so we agree before I build.

  3. III

    A working version every week

    Something you can use, not a demo, with each decision written down as it's made.

  4. IV

    Handover

    Tests and documentation come with the code, and I stay on for fixes and the next version.

I studied electronics, control systems and embedded design at NTUA, and I'm now studying Computer Engineering at the University of Nicosia. Alongside, I build the full-stack software that brings hardware to life: I run a home lab, ship production apps, and care about the decisions behind the code as much as the code itself.
